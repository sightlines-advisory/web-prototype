Five steps to a confident start on climate reporting

You already have a ledger, a reporting rhythm and a business you know how to describe. Start there.

Sean Rugers | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 2026Method · Draft for author approval

Climate reporting under AASB S2 is a financial reporting obligation. If you're in finance, much of the discipline is familiar: define your reporting boundary, document your judgements, reconcile the numbers and keep an audit file.

What's new is the subject matter, and how much of the information sits outside the finance function. You need contributions from operations, risk, strategy, procurement and leadership. But you don't need to start from a blank page.

We've put together Five steps to a confident start on climate reporting for finance and reporting teams in Group 3 entities preparing for their first reporting year, and Group 2 entities already in theirs. It's a practical self-service guide to the first decisions and actions—not a substitute for the full reporting requirements.

The five steps

  1. Understand what's being asked. Confirm your reporting group and timing, understand the four disclosure pillars, and identify who needs to be involved. It's mostly about how climate affects your business, not just emissions.
  2. Take stock of what you have. Your annual report, website, strategy, risk register, asset records and supplier information are a useful first draft. Ask management to confirm and correct it.
  3. Run it like a financial reporting project. Document positions and materiality judgements, assign owners, build your evidence trail and plan for assurance.
  4. Start emissions at the ledger. Use existing financial records to identify energy, fuel and purchasing activity. Work from quantities where available, and refine estimates as better data arrives.
  5. Look for the opportunity and the value. Connect the material risks and opportunities to costs, revenue, capital, resilience and performance. Decide what follows.

What you can do this month

Start by confirming your group and first reporting year. Bring together the business information you already use. Name the people who own the evidence. Record the judgements you need to make. Then identify the gaps that genuinely require more work.

The guide includes self-service starters for each step, a readiness self-check and options for keeping the work in-house or getting targeted help.

Do it yourself, or get support?

If you've got the capacity, use the guide to make a start yourself. If you're stuck on a judgement or evidence gap, bring in support for that issue. If you need a coordinated start, our AASB S2 Fast Track works from what you already have, so management confirms rather than creates.

My suggestion: start with the information you trust, write down what you don't yet know, and give the right people clear questions to answer. That creates a more auditable disclosure process—and a better basis for business decisions.
