# Sightlines Insights — editorial and interface rules

**Status:** staging working standard  
**Owner:** Sightlines Advisory  
**Apply this standard whenever an Insight is drafted, added to staging or prepared for publication.**

## 1. Purpose

Each Insight must help a reader to:

1. discover a relevant question quickly;
2. explore related articles and useful pathways;
3. use or share a quotable idea responsibly; and
4. find the article again through search engines and AI answer systems.

Treat every article as a durable, individually discoverable professional resource, not a news post.

## 2. Required article metadata

Every article has:

- title and plain-language standfirst;
- content type;
- one to three approved Topic facets (including Climate value where the article connects climate action to business performance);
- AASB S2 and transition-planning facets where relevant;
- one or two maturity labels, where relevant;
- approved author/byline, date and reading time;
- source, attribution, image-credit and quotation constraints where applicable.

Use a stable article URL and descriptive title/meta description for publication.

## 3. Controlled facets

### Topic — select one to three

Use only approved Topics. The current controlled set includes:

- Climate disclosure
- Enterprise value
- Climate value
- Governance
- **Data**
- **AI**

Add new Topics only when they will be used repeatedly and can be understood without internal jargon.

### Content type — select exactly one

- Explainer
- Insight
- Method
- Guide
- Research
- Observed case
- Case study
- Interview

### AASB S2 — select up to two

- Governance
- Strategy
- Risk management
- Metrics & targets

### Transition planning — select up to two

- Foundations
- Stakeholders
- Implementation
- Accountability

### Maturity — select one or two where relevant

- First disclosure
- Strengthening disclosures
- Major decisions

Show content type and maturity prominently. Present AASB S2 and transition-planning tags as **Helps with** metadata.

## 4. Tile system

- Standard tile proportion: **4:5 portrait**.
- The visual cover occupies about **42–45%** of the card.
- Typographic tiles are for Explainers, Guides and Methods: Explainers are light/evidence-led; Guides are practical and action-oriented; Methods are ink-navy/dark and structured.
- Photographic tiles are for Case studies and Interviews. Keep the heading below the image; do not heavily tint it.
- Sightlines illustrated/infographic tiles are for data, changing conditions, evidence pathways, analytical pathways and the Climate Value Framework.
- Use one clear analytical proposition per tile, not a dense mini-infographic.
- **Tile backgrounds must use only assets from the approved Sightlines Google Drive libraries:** [library 1](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1yGUPYAOqgCYoLfPVFqhxR3DmeWLTn85t?usp=drive_link) or [library 2](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1cI_sI9eprvQSzB5rBrKEDuFucUtN04UY?usp=drive_link). Do not source, generate or substitute a background image outside these libraries without explicit approval.
- Retain any required credit, licence or usage constraint recorded with the chosen asset.

### Colour meanings

- Ink navy: system, method, authority
- Slate blue: evidence and context
- Copper: Insight
- Value green: action, implementation, value and applied cases
- Warm orange: thresholds, urgency or restrained emphasis

## 5. Observed cases

Use **Observed case** whenever Sightlines does not have an approved client relationship or permission to present the organisation as a case study.

- State that it is an independent public-source observation.
- Do not imply client status, endorsement, permission or an assessment of performance.
- Use licensed, generated or sufficiently non-identifying contextual imagery unless reuse rights are confirmed.
- Include an appropriate independent-observation disclaimer in the article.

## 6. Authorship, quotation and sources

**Institutional byline policy:**

- Use **[Approved author name] | Sightlines Advisory** when an identifiable team member led the writing, research synthesis or professional interpretation and has approved the attribution. All current Explainers are attributed to **Susan McHattie | Sightlines Advisory**; the Five Steps Method is attributed to **Sean Rugers | Sightlines Advisory**.
- Use **Sightlines Advisory** alone only for a genuinely collective, institutional resource with no agreed lead author. Do not use it to avoid confirming authorship.
- Where appropriate, add **With contributions from [name]** only after the contributor approves. Do not invent co-authors or imply endorsement.
- A named byline identifies editorial responsibility, not sole ownership of research or claims. Verify source attribution independently.
- Present the author consistently on the landing card and article, followed by publication month/year, estimated reading time and audience maturity.
- Changes to authorship require the relevant author's approval; retain draft/staging status until approved.
- Mark approved `PULL QUOTE` and `SHAREABLE INSIGHT` text explicitly in source material.
- Flag non-quotable language, required attribution, image credits and mandatory source links.
- Do not publish unapproved material, client claims or external-source imagery without the required permissions.

## 7. Navigation and search

- Lead with curated editorial pathways; do not use a large tag cloud.
- Search should match title, standfirst, Topic, content type, maturity, AASB S2 and transition-planning facets.
- Use guided pathways such as First disclosure, Strengthening disclosures, Major decisions, Governance and Strategy.
- Secondary filters are progressively disclosed rather than presented all at once.
- **Content options sit on one single line directly below the search bar at half their prior visual size.** Do not wrap them into a multi-row desktop control set.

## 8. Article recommendations

At the bottom of each current article, show **two** curated related articles with one-sentence reasons to read them. Add a third, complementary perspective only after the library includes sufficient published Insight, Method or Observed case material to make it meaningful.

Never recommend the current article. Do not show invented recommendations, empty modules, endless scrolling or generic carousels.

## 9. Publication check

Before publication, confirm this rules file has been applied, then verify:

- approved copy, byline, date and taxonomy;
- tile treatment, rights and attribution;
- quotation and source rules;
- observed-case/client-relationship framing;
- stable URL, title and metadata;
- only genuine related-article recommendations.

## 10. Evidentiary distinctions within articles

Use natural sentences, not repeated badges or new section headings, to make the status of information clear:

- **Established evidence:** attribute claims to named primary sources, with uncertainty and relevant dates.
- **Requirements:** write directly, for example, “AASB S2 requires …”, specifying applicability when necessary.
- **Interpretation:** signal the author’s reasoning through language such as “For businesses, this suggests …” rather than attributing conclusions to the source.
- **Illustration:** identify fictional companies and hypothetical outcomes in the narrative, for example, “Junction Freight Services, our fictional Australian distribution business …”.

Do not introduce headings or conspicuous labels merely to classify these statements. Preserve the author’s original framing and voice; these distinctions supplement primary-source checks.

## 11. Standard reading template and article journeys

- Every article uses an editorial eyebrow, title, plain-language standfirst, author byline, month/year, estimated reading time and maturity label in that order.
- Add one or more contextual links where the prose genuinely connects to another published article. Use descriptive link text; avoid keyword stuffing and circular cross-links.
- End with **Continue exploring**, two relevant articles, short explanations, and **Return to the Insights library** linking to the library anchor. Do not add a third recommendation until editorial diversity warrants it.
- Format all article tables with a consistent header, top-left aligned cells, clear borders and restrained zebra stripes. For narrow viewports, scroll the table horizontally within a keyboard-focusable labelled region; do not squeeze multi-column tables to illegibility.
- Attribute external claims in natural prose to named primary sources and provide source links or a references section when approved. Do not fabricate links, quotations or bibliographic details. Staging-only source notes flag verification still required.
- Preserve authorial voice, approved source text and editorial distinctions between requirements, established evidence, interpretation and illustration.
- Check 360px mobile, tablet and desktop widths, keyboard focus, long titles and table overflow before release.

## 12. Approved editorial components and research controls (November 2026)

- **Insight** is the single article type for authored interpretation of research. “Research” may describe the evidence base, but must not appear as a second article type. The Insight tile uses a Mist Grey uppercase AASB S2 heading, Steel Blue headline and Amber half-line.
- **Evidence Figure:** white background, Mist Grey borders, Steel Blue neutral measures, Green Copper opportunity or value, Copper constraints. Cite primary source, sample, geography, date, denominator and material qualifications. Do not repeat each plotted statistic verbatim in adjacent prose.
- **Illustrative Company:** no enclosing border; small Green Copper label, semibold Deep Navy company name, 48px by 3px Green Copper rule. Avoid repeating the name in the first sentence. Fictional outcomes must not be described as observed evidence.
- **Lens List:** icon-led questions, labels without hyphens, source note; not a table.
- **Five Steps Method:** five separated 3px lines above numbers, four Steel Blue and final Green Copper.
- **Closing question:** when the article ends with a substantive management question, present it as the final prominent callout using `<div class="article-callout"><strong>Question text?</strong></div>` (before any source note and related readings). This is the approved Article 2 pattern, now also applied to Article 1. Use exactly one closing-question callout; retain the article's wording and avoid manufacturing a question where the ending is a statement or call to action. The callout must remain accessible and use the shared `article-callout` styling; do not add inline styling or a new one-off CSS class.
- **Research verification:** check the primary report for exact measures, respondent definition, denominator, fieldwork dates, relative versus absolute percentages, limitations and reuse rights. Treat reported associations as non-causal. Flag source-cover artwork pending rights clearance rather than substituting an unapproved image.
- **Publication gate:** verify 360px, 768px and desktop layouts, keyboard focus, accessible figures, valid links, byline approval, image rights and remove staging noindex only after publication approval.
