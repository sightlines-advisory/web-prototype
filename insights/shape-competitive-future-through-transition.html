EXPLAINER · AASB S2: STRATEGY

How can your business shape a competitive future through the transition?

Sightlines Advisory · November 2026 · 2 min read · First disclosure

Businesses influence transition to a low carbon economy through the products you offer, the assets you replace, the suppliers you choose and the capabilities you develop, and the implementation timetable you choose.

Those business choices occur within changing markets, technologies and institutions. Understanding how these interact helps leaders explain where the business is positioned and what its projections of future performance assume, including how innovation, economic and institutional change enable your future performance.

For company analysis, three practical questions shape your competitive strength.

DimensionWhat management needs to understand
DirectionHow do policy, standards and institutional arrangements influence the conditions for doing business?
DemandWhat are customers prepared to specify, purchase or finance?
InnovationWhich technologies, capabilities and business models could change the company’s competitive advantage?

Your answers to these questions shape transition strategy.

Different businesses have different ways to participate

Connected Ground Civil creates value through project execution, estimating discipline, skilled teams and equipment productivity. Co-founder and Managing Director Emma Davis examines whether changing infrastructure specifications create demand the company can serve profitably.

Connected’s position depends on more than technical capability. Tender terms, workforce availability, equipment requirements and payment timing also affect the commercial proposition.

Southern Cross Commercial Finance creates value through lending. CEO Rebecca Chen considers how customers’ changing needs affect demand for finance, while Chief Risk Officer Amira Hassan examines borrower capacity and portfolio exposures.

Southern’s influence lies partly in how it understands customers and assesses lending propositions. The financial consequences depend on credit quality, funding costs and the performance of the portfolio.

Questions for your disclosure

  • Governance. How does the board oversee significant changes in markets, technology and policy?
  • Strategy. Where could those changes affect the company’s competitive position and financial outlook?
  • Risk management. How are external dependencies, uncertainty and potential concentrations assessed?
  • Metrics and targets. Which measures explain the company’s position and progress against its stated commitments?

AASB S2 connects climate-related matters with strategy, business models, value chains and financial effects. The reporting standard asks you to explain the relationships between climate and the standard pillars. More importantly, the answers are critical to future business success.
