Customers need to see the value in your environmental claims

How substantiated environmental claims and purchasing requirements can affect customer value.

Susan McHattie | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 20262 min read (estimated)First disclosure

Explore this subject further: Suppliers need a practical path from expectations to delivery.

A customer choosing between two products may care about emissions and still choose on price, reliability or running costs. A business customer may express support for sustainability, then make its purchasing decision through a detailed tender specification that is more likely to include environmental considerations in future.

Australian research offers a local perspective on how environmental performance enters buying decisions for consumers and business and government customers.

Consumers

The Consumer Policy Research Centre is an independent, not for profit consumer think tank focused on creating fairer markets for Australians. One of their focus areas in Sustainability is ending misleading green claims at point of sale and promoting the information consumers need to make good decisions. The Misused and Confused study published with Energy Consumers Australia in 2025 found strong demand for better information about large appliances.

These findings concern appliances and require testing before applying them to other markets.

Marianne Campbell, the project’s lead researcher and CPRC Senior Research and Engagement Advisor, explained that consumers need:

“clear details on how long a product will last and whether it can be easily repaired”

Marianne Campbell, September 2025.

This suggests an opportunity to make your environmental performance tangible. You can explain your product’s operating costs, expected life, maintenance requirements and substantiated environmental attributes. And test which information changes customer choices.

Business and government customers

Business customers are turning sustainability expectations into supplier surveys, tender specifications and contractual commitments.

Telstra reported that 168 supplier contracts included climate clauses at the end of FY2025, linked to more than A$3.4 billion in annual spend. The clauses commit suppliers to disclosing and reducing absolute emissions. That is one buyer’s practice, demonstrating a specific commercial requirement.

Three questions for leaders

  • Which environmental attributes appear in our customers’ purchasing criteria and contracts?
  • What do sales results, tender feedback and customer conversations show about the importance of substantiated environmental claims?
  • Can we demonstrate the benefit and substantiate the claim?
