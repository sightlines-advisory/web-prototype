Which stakeholder expectations affect your business?

Sightlines Advisory · November 2026 · 2 min read · First disclosure

A customer’s purchasing requirements can affect revenue. A lender’s assessment can affect funding. Employees influence capability and delivery. Suppliers shape costs, reliability and the products your company can offer.

Understanding these expectations helps leaders see where changing conditions might affect enterprise value.

For Fieldwork Technical & Digital, customer relationships, specialist knowledge and intellectual property sustain the business. Founder and CEO Alex Morgan needs to understand whether customers’ changing requirements create demand for different services, and whether Fieldwork has the people and capabilities to meet it.

For Mallee Harvest Foods, Managing Director Helen Russo brings a different perspective. Product quality, growing conditions and input availability shape what the business can supply and earn.

Four example stakeholder perspectives to investigate

StakeholderWhat external evidence suggestsWhat the company needs to establish
Investors and financiersClimate investment appetite exists alongside scrutiny of risk, returns and delivery conditions.Which assumptions about future earnings, assets and funding require stronger evidence?
CustomersEnvironmental expectations can appear through product information, purchasing specifications and contracts.Which requirements influence actual purchasing and commercial terms?
EmployeesEnvironmental credentials matter to many younger workers alongside financial security and working conditions.Where do expectations affect recruitment, retention and capability?
SuppliersSmaller businesses face varying levels of understanding, resources and control over assets.Which dependencies could constrain the company’s plans or create opportunities for collaboration?

These interpretations draw on Australian investor research, consumer research, workforce surveys and small-business energy research. Each source describes a particular population and context.

Fieldwork’s commercial records could show whether environmental questions are becoming tender requirements. Mallee’s customer correspondence could reveal whether buyers are changing product specifications or supply expectations. Leaders can then examine the financial implications.

Questions for your disclosure

  • Governance. How do material stakeholder expectations reach management and the board?
  • Strategy. Which expectations could affect the business model, revenue, costs or access to resources?
  • Risk management. How does the company assess their significance and monitor changes?
  • Metrics and targets. What evidence supports the relationships described, and how are relevant commitments measured?

Sightlines Advisory uses the Climate Value Framework © to connect stakeholder evidence with value drivers and financial consequences. This helps leaders focus on the expectations that warrant attention, explain why they matter and develop responses.
