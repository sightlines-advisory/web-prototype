EXPLAINER · AASB S2: STRATEGY

Which stakeholder expectations affect your business?

Investigate which expectations of investors, customers, employees and suppliers affect value.

Susan McHattie | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 20263 min read (estimated)First disclosure

Share on LinkedIn

Explore this subject further: Investors want to understand how your business will create value; Customers need to see the value in your environmental claims; Employees want credible action and a future they can work towards; Suppliers need a practical path from expectations to delivery.

Investors, customers, employees and suppliers each influence how your business creates and sustains value.

Their expectations can affect access to capital, customer demand, workforce capability, supply reliability and the cost of doing business.

But not every expectation is material to every enterprise. Leaders need to understand which expectations are changing, how they could affect business performance, and what information supports that assessment.

The question for leaders is which stakeholder expectations and behaviours could change enterprise value.

ILLUSTRATIVE COMPANY

Fieldwork Technical & Digital

For this fictional technical and digital services business, specialist knowledge, intellectual property and customer relationships are central to enterprise value. Founder and CEO Alex Morgan needs to understand whether changing customer expectations will create demand for new services, and whether Fieldwork can recruit, develop or partner for the capabilities required to deliver them.

Leadership question: Which emerging customer requirements mean we need to strengthen our service offering, and what customer insight justifies investing in new capabilities?

ILLUSTRATIVE COMPANY

Mallee Harvest Foods

For this fictional Australian food business, product quality, reliable supply and relationships with growers and customers underpin performance. Managing Director Helen Russo needs to understand how changing buyer requirements, input availability and growing conditions could affect supply commitments and margins.

Leadership question: Which customer and supplier expectations could cause us to change our product mix, sourcing arrangements or investment priorities?

External research can identify emerging expectations, but it cannot establish how material they are to an individual company. That requires information from the enterprise and direct engagement with stakeholders.

Which expectations deserve attention?

Leaders can assess stakeholder expectations through three questions:

  • Relevance: Does the expectation affect an important source of enterprise value or a dependency on which the business relies?
  • Consequence: Could it materially change revenue, costs, capital requirements, operational reliability or future growth?
  • Business information: What do contracts, commercial decisions, enterprise records and direct stakeholder engagement tell us about its significance?

This approach helps distinguish an emerging expectation from an established commercial requirement. It also identifies where further engagement is needed before drawing conclusions.

Questions for your disclosure

  • Governance: How do material stakeholder expectations reach leaders and the board, and who is responsible for responding?
  • Strategy: Which expectations could affect the business model, value drivers, revenue, costs or access to resources?
  • Risk management: How does the business identify, assess and monitor expectations that could become material climate-related risks or opportunities?
  • Metrics and targets: What evidence supports the conclusions, and how will the business assess whether its response is working?

These are suggested leadership questions, not a substitute for the specific AASB S2 disclosure requirements.

The Climate Value Framework connects stakeholder information and insights with enterprise value drivers, risks, opportunities and financial consequences.

Leaders can use this understanding to determine which relationships deserve closer attention, where further engagement is needed, and which responses could protect or create value.

It is not possible to satisfy every expectation. Leaders will recognise and sustain the relationships that underpin company value.
