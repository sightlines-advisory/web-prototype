EXPLAINER · AASB S2: STRATEGY

What will enable your business to deliver its transition?

Your transition depends on capabilities within the business and conditions you can influence beyond it. Seven lenses help leaders identify what will enable delivery, where support is available and which dependencies need attention.

Susan McHattie | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 20264 min read (estimated)First disclosure · Major decisions

Explore the strategic opportunity: What can you see that your competitors have missed?.

A business can recognise a valuable opportunity through climate transition and still lack the conditions needed to deliver it. Investment depends on people, finance, technology, customers, suppliers and infrastructure. Some conditions are within the business's control. Others depend on partners, institutions or wider markets.

Leaders can strengthen their capacity to act by identifying these dependencies early, developing internal capabilities and engaging with the organisations that can help. The practical question is which conditions matter most to enterprise performance and how to improve them.

Seven lenses for enabling transition

The following lenses are drawn from Stella Whittaker's adaptation of the CLIMATEFIT Maturity Assessment Model, referenced in Performance or Compliance: your approach to climate risk matters. They provide a way to investigate both the conditions around the enterprise and its capacity to respond.

Exposure

Which climate influences could affect operations, customers, suppliers and assets?

Industry structure

How do competition, bargaining power and supply relationships influence the company’s options? What influence does the company have over industry stance and structure?

Policy landscape

Which rules, standards, incentives, industry and regional roadmaps and public commitments affect the business? What internal rules, standards, incentives and policies enable the company to respond quickly to new conditions?

Market and finance

What do customers and financiers require? What financial capacity does the company have? What company intelligence provides evidence for meeting customer, lender and insurance shifting requirements?

Technology

Which capabilities are available and suitable for the company’s operating circumstances? Which new capabilities and innovations can the company develop?

Education

What new knowledge and skills are needed across leaders, the workforce and partners? What knowledge and skills within the company enable competitive advantage in a low carbon economy?

Culture

How do established practices, leadership and relationships affect the company’s capacity to respond? What new practices, leadership and relationships would create new value?

Source: Performance or Compliance: your approach to climate risk matters, Susan McHattie and Stella Whittaker, p. 17 and reference 35. Seven lenses drawn from Stella Whittaker’s adaptation of the CLIMATEFIT Maturity Assessment Model.

Turning enabling conditions into practical choices

The seven lenses help leaders connect external opportunities with capabilities they can build. Examples include:

  • Finance and investment: Use asset condition, energy performance and investment appraisal to prioritise projects. The Australian Government's grants and funding guide identifies grants, loans and co-financing options; the Clean Energy Finance Corporation supports eligible clean-energy investment.
  • Technology and infrastructure: Commission energy assessments, test equipment suitability and consider network or site constraints before committing capital. NSW's business equipment upgrades arrangements illustrate how approved suppliers and incentives can support efficiency improvements.
  • Skills and organisational capability: Build practical knowledge in procurement, finance, operations and project delivery, with clear accountability for implementation. NSW's Skills for Net Zero initiative provides an example of external workforce support.
  • Industry and partnerships: Work with customers, suppliers, industry associations and local partners to develop workable specifications, shared investment cases and supply capacity. Regional Workforce Transition Officers offer support to employers and communities in designated regions.
  • Planning and evidence: Establish reliable asset, energy and commercial information, then test the costs, benefits and dependencies of proposed actions. NSW's Net Zero Planning Grant is an example of targeted planning assistance for eligible businesses.

Program eligibility, application windows and funding change. These examples illustrate types of assistance; leaders should check current guidelines before relying on support in an investment decision.

Different business models expose different dependencies. Two fictional Australian enterprises illustrate how leaders could investigate the conditions for delivery.

ILLUSTRATIVE COMPANY

Clearwater Infrastructure Services

This fictional infrastructure services business depends on reliable essential services, long-lived assets, technical expertise and funding for maintenance and renewal. Its transition opportunities could include more efficient equipment, resilient infrastructure and improved asset performance.

Aroha Wilson — Chief Executive Officer considers how customer requirements, electricity infrastructure, specialist contractors and technology availability influence what the business can deliver. Peter Okafor — Finance Director examines investment timing, financing capacity and the cost of maintaining service continuity.

Leadership question: Which improvements can we deliver with our existing capabilities, and where do we need customer commitments, specialist partners or finance to make the investment viable?

Clearwater could begin with an asset and energy performance assessment, then test equipment upgrades against contract terms, capital availability and operational requirements. External incentives may improve a business case, but the commercial benefit depends on asset performance and the terms of investment.

ILLUSTRATIVE COMPANY

Community Horizons Services

This fictional community services provider creates value through dependable care and training services. Its performance depends on skilled people, suitable premises, reliable transport, client access and funding agreements.

David Nguyen — Chief Executive Officer is considering how to reduce energy costs and strengthen service continuity without diverting scarce resources from frontline delivery.

Leadership question: How can we improve our premises and service resilience while working within funding agreements, lease arrangements and workforce capacity?

Community Horizons could review energy use and heat exposure at service locations, engage landlords about building upgrades and work with funders and training partners on feasible improvements. Its ability to act may depend more on lease terms, relationships and workforce planning than on access to new technology.

These examples show why leaders need to distinguish the conditions they control, those they can influence and those they must accommodate. The response may involve developing a capability, negotiating a contract, joining an industry initiative or adjusting the timing of investment.

Which enabling conditions deserve attention first?

Start with the initiatives that matter most to future enterprise value. Identify the capabilities and dependencies essential to their success, the evidence available and the actions that could make delivery more feasible. Assign responsibility for monitoring conditions outside the business, particularly where infrastructure, policy, finance or partners could change the investment case.

At Sightlines Advisory, the Climate Value Framework connects these enabling conditions with the enterprise's value drivers, climate risks and opportunities, and financial choices. It helps leaders test which responses are feasible, what must change to deliver them and where collaboration could create or protect value.
