EXPLAINER · AASB S2: STRATEGY

What support is available for transition?

Examine the external and internal conditions that enable business transition.

Susan McHattie | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 20263 min read (estimated)First disclosure

Explore this subject further: How can your business shape a competitive future through the transition?.

A company’s plans to shift to the low carbon, resilient economy depend on conditions inside and outside the business. Exposure, policy, industry structure, finance, workforce capability, infrastructure, technology and customer demand can each affect what is feasible and when. Understanding these enabling conditions helps leaders plan.

Seven lenses for understanding the company’s position and internal and external enablers drawn from Stella Whittaker’s adaptation of the CLIMATEFIT Maturity Assessment Model.

Exposure

Which climate influences could affect operations, customers, suppliers and assets?

Industry structure

How do competition, bargaining power and supply relationships influence the company’s options? What influence does the company have over industry stance and structure?

Policy landscape

Which rules, standards, incentives, industry and regional roadmaps and public commitments affect the business? What internal rules, standards, incentives and policies enable the company to respond quickly to new conditions?

Market and finance

What do customers and financiers require? What financial capacity does the company have? What company intelligence provides evidence for meeting customer, lender and insurance shifting requirements?

Technology

Which capabilities are available and suitable for the company’s operating circumstances? Which new capabilities and innovations can the company develop?

Education

What new knowledge and skills are needed across leaders, the workforce and partners? What knowledge and skills within the company enable competitive advantage in a low carbon economy?

Culture

How do established practices, leadership and relationships affect the company’s capacity to respond? What new practices, leadership and relationships would create new value?

Source: Performance or Compliance: your approach to climate risk matters, Susan McHattie and Stella Whittaker, p. 17 and reference 35. Seven lenses drawn from Stella Whittaker’s adaptation of the CLIMATEFIT Maturity Assessment Model.

ILLUSTRATIVE COMPANY

Clearwater Infrastructure Services

The company depends on reliable essential services and long lived assets. CEO Aroha Wilson examines how its capacity relates to infrastructure dependencies, technical capability and funding. Finance Director Peter Okafor considers what those dependencies mean for financial flexibility.

ILLUSTRATIVE COMPANY

Community Horizons Services

Its business model is different. Its care and training services depend on people, premises, client access and funding agreements. CEO David Nguyen needs to understand where the organisation can influence those external conditions and where it relies on others.

Distinguishing what leaders control, can influence or must accommodate helps make the disclosure explanation more precise, and more importantly, helps shape transition strategy.

Questions for your disclosure

  • Governance. How are significant dependencies and capability limitations brought to the board’s attention?
  • Strategy. Which conditions underpin the company’s plans, resilience and financial outlook?
  • Risk management. How are those assumptions assessed and changes monitored within existing processes?
  • Metrics and targets. Do targets and progress statements explain the dependencies relevant to their achievement?

AASB S2 calls for disclosure of key assumptions, dependencies and resourcing in any transition plan that exists.
