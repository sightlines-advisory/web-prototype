Follow the value: making climate analysis useful to management

A fictional example of an Insight that turns a broad topic into an executive-level management question.

Draft example5 min read5 November 2026

Climate analysis becomes useful when it can be followed through the enterprise. Not just from a hazard or policy signal to a risk label, but through to the assets, customers, costs, capital or capabilities that make performance possible.

That sounds obvious. In practice, the chain is often broken. Operational teams may understand a site-level constraint. Finance may model a cost. Strategy may see a market opening. The connection between them remains implicit.

Use a simple value map

A practical starting point is to map how the business creates and protects value today. Then test how changing conditions could alter each part of that map.

  • Could an operating condition affect output, reliability or cost?
  • Could customer requirements change the terms of demand or access to a market?
  • Could a financing, insurance or asset decision change the cost of capital or resilience?

These are not abstract climate questions. They are management questions, expressed in the language of the business.

The objective is not to force certainty. It is to identify the decisions that should be better informed before they become urgent.

Give management a decision path

When the value consequence is clear, action can become specific: a data gap to close, an assumption to test, an investment option to review or an owner to nominate. That is a much more useful output than a catalogue of generic risks.
