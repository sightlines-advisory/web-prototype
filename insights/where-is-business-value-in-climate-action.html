Where is the business value in climate action?

International research released by global climate consultancy South Pole in October 2026 highlights a significant gap between corporate climate action and direct impact on stronger business performance. For CEOs and Managing Directors, the opportunity is to make value creation the starting point.

Susan McHattie | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 20265 min read (estimated)First disclosure · Strengthening disclosures

Explore the foundations: How do climate risks affect company value?

The value delivery gap

Nine in ten senior business leaders surveyed say their organisations have active climate initiatives. Yet only 12% meet all four South Pole foundations for translating climate action into business value.

South Pole’s 2026 Net Zero Report: Climate Action as Business Value draws on the experience and insight of 600 senior decision-makers across Europe and the United States. The key message: a striking gap between activity and impact.

Just 43% report having a formal, approved climate strategy with implementation well underway. Only 23% say that at least three-quarters of their climate-related business cases secured CFO or investment committee approval over the preceding two years.

For business leaders, this raises an important question: Are we approaching climate transition in a way that strengthens the business?

Value creation changes the conversation

South Pole’s research offers encouraging evidence of what is working.

Their research identifies four foundational criteria or building blocks for translating climate action into business value: deciding where to act, securing commitment, mobilising delivery, and measuring results.

These are reported associations, rather than proof of cause and effect. Nevertheless, they suggest that integrating climate into business strategy, investment and performance management is associated with stronger commercial outcomes.

Consider the opportunities already emerging across businesses:

  • A manufacturer investing in energy efficiency reduces costs while improving productivity and strengthening its competitive position.
  • A supplier responding to changing customer requirements protects existing contracts while gaining access to new markets.
  • An infrastructure operator investing in resilience protects service continuity while improving asset performance and long-term financial flexibility.

Each example drives to a business outcome. Each example shows how companies can respond to changing climate conditions and achieve their business outcomes. Each opportunity must be tested against costs, evidence, capability and the value the enterprise can realistically capture.

Start with the business you have

Every organisation creates value differently.

Some depend on efficient assets and reliable supply chains. Others depend on intellectual property, customer relationships, specialist skills, trusted brands or access to finance.

The starting point for a CEO is understanding which of these drivers matter most, what assumptions underpin their future performance and where changing conditions create opportunities to do better.

Climate transition brings new pressures and possibilities. Customer preferences change. Technologies become more competitive. Energy and insurance costs shift. Physical climate conditions affect assets, suppliers and markets.

These changes can undermine established sources of value. They can also create demand for new products, services, capabilities and partnerships.

The leadership task is to recognise those possibilities early, test them and make informed decisions about where to invest.

From insight to investment

At Sightlines Advisory, our Climate Value Framework provides an enterprise-first approach to these decisions.

We work through five connected questions:

  1. What creates value in your business today?
  2. Which climate risks and opportunities affect those sources of value?
  3. What does your own business evidence tell you?
  4. How might the financial impacts change under plausible future scenarios?
  5. Which responses create and protect the greatest value, and when?

This approach connects climate analysis with the decisions executives already make about customers, products, operations, capital and growth.

It also provides a stronger foundation for climate-related financial disclosures, using evidence developed for management decisions.
