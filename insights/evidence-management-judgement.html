Evidence, management and judgement: three things a board needs to see

A fictional example of a concise governance-focused Insight for a board and executive audience.

Draft example4 min read5 November 2026

Boards do not need every data point. They do need confidence that material climate-related decisions rest on an understandable chain of evidence, management analysis and accountable judgement.

The distinction is important. Evidence tells us what is known. Analysis interprets what it could mean for the company. Judgement is where management chooses an assumption, response or priority in the face of uncertainty.

Make the chain visible

A short, well-structured briefing can show all three. It can identify the source material behind a conclusion, set out the assumption being tested and state the management choice that follows. This makes challenge more constructive and assurance more practical.

Good governance is not about removing judgement. It is about making the basis and ownership of judgement clear.

Focus attention where it matters

When information is structured this way, directors can spend less time looking for the source of a statement and more time asking the questions that matter: what could change our view, what must happen next, and who is accountable?

That approach can also make internal work more efficient. Teams know which evidence needs to be strengthened, which decisions are open and which questions require escalation.
