Climate risk disclosures: what do diligent directors ask?

Climate disclosures offer directors an opportunity to understand what could change enterprise value, test strategic assumptions and strengthen investment decisions.

Susan McHattie | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 20265 min read (estimated)First disclosure · Major decisions

Share on LinkedIn

How will our changing climate affect our business’s ability to create and protect value?

Sightlines Advisory ask that question when a board is preparing for mandatory climate risk disclosures. We also recommend it for boards considering a major investment, a new product or a change to their operating model.

Why? Consider a facility renewal. The business case may depend on reliable power, access to freight routes and customer demand. The board needs to understand the assumptions underpinning the investment, how conditions might plausibly change over its useful life, and where those changes could adversely affect projected results. What options would the business have to respond, and when would it need to act?

This is how climate enters enterprise strategy. It tests the assumptions behind choices the board is making about where to compete, what to invest in and how to sustain performance.

What directors are being asked to do

In his foreword to version 2 of the AICD’s A director’s guide to mandatory climate reporting, ASIC Chair Joe Longo described the shift to mandatory climate disclosure as “the biggest change to corporate reporting in a generation”.

For directors of entities required to prepare a sustainability report, the Corporations Act 2001 requires a directors’ declaration.

For financial years commencing between 1 January 2025 and 31 December 2027, directors must declare that, in their opinion, the entity has taken reasonable steps to ensure that the sustainability report, other than the declaration itself, complies with the Act and AASB S2.

For financial years commencing on or after 1 January 2028, the declaration addresses whether, in the directors’ opinion, the rest of the report complies.

Directors need to understand the requirements, guide and oversee the work, understand the implications for the business and follow through to ensure that conclusions are adequately supported.

Four questions for the board

We use the four AASB S2 pillars to structure the conversation. You can apply these questions to your first disclosure or to business decisions that matter.

Board checklist

Use these checkboxes to track the questions your board has considered. Select a box to mark a question complete; select it again to clear it. Selections are temporary and are not saved or submitted.

0 of 4 steps selected

1

Governance

What is the role of our board, our committees and management?

Who owns the work in management? Which teams need to be involved? Which committee is best positioned to oversee the work? What matters require board attention? How do we as directors satisfy ourselves that the work is progressing and the conclusions are robust?

2

Strategy

Which climate influences could alter how we create value, and what would we do differently?

How might plausible futures affect customer demand, operating costs, cash flow and investments? How are we engaging customers and suppliers on risks, opportunities and transition? Which external mitigation and adaptation measures can we reasonably rely on? Where does our business need to take the lead to protect and create value?

3

Risk and opportunity management

How does management determine which risks and opportunities matter most?

What company experience and records support the assessment? Where is there uncertainty? How are the findings embedded into existing risk and planning processes? How does management weigh competing perspectives and uncertainties, and what judgements require board consideration?

4

Metrics and targets

What will tell us whether our response is working?

Which measures should the board track? What targets describe our ambition and strategy? How will progress be assessed, and what would trigger a change in course?

These questions are developed by Sightlines Advisory, drawing on AASB S2, paragraphs 5–7, 8–23, 24–26 and 27–37.

The checklist in practice

Consider how these questions could inform a decision to invest in a new product line.

ILLUSTRATIVE COMPANY

Mallee Harvest Foods

Our fictional Australian food producer supplies processed vegetable products and ingredients to supermarkets, food manufacturers and foodservice customers. Product mix, factory throughput and reliable delivery contribute to its margins.

As customers strengthen their procurement requirements and introduce climate considerations, the company is investigating a new product line. The opportunity might justify a trial now and a larger investment once customer demand, production requirements and margins are better understood.

The board can ask management to:

  • Confirm what customers will specify in tenders and what this means for product design, sourcing and production.
  • Review the criteria and conditions for committing further capital in light of climate influences.
  • Agree what information and evidence management will bring back to support the next investment decision.

This is strategic analysis with a climate lens. It demonstrates how governance, an understanding of risk and opportunity, and an assessment of changing conditions and markets can inform a strategic choice, the funding decision and measures used to assess success.

How Sightlines Advisory builds director confidence

We bring strategy, governance and financial reporting expertise to this work.

Through the Climate Value Framework, we start with how the business creates value. We use existing financial, operational and commercial records to test the impact of climate influence.

We prioritise analysis where it matters: to challenge assumptions underpinning an investment, address material uncertainties or support disclosure. We build the audit trail throughout the process, anticipating assurance requirements.

This approach means:

  • Directors can see which conclusions are supported by company records, where management has used estimates and which assumptions still need testing. The same evidence can support the board paper, the investment decision and the disclosure.
  • Finance and operations teams can focus their efforts on material risks and opportunities, reduce duplication in information collection and assess and schedule response options.

Sightlines Advisory helps your management team plan and deliver an efficient disclosure process that produces the insight and evidence directors need.
