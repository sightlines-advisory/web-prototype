Suppliers need a practical path from expectations to delivery

Susan McHattie | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 2026Draft for review

A supplier can support your climate ambition and still struggle to meet the next information request or fund an equipment upgrade.

The commercial question is whether your suppliers can deliver the changes your business depends on, and what would make that possible.

Energy Consumers Australia’s 2026 Small Business Energy Consumer Survey helps explain the circumstances of smaller businesses.

  • 48% supported Australia’s energy transition and 29% opposed it.
  • Only 37% understood the steps their business needed to take.
  • 61% reported taking no action to reduce emissions, while 22% were considering or planning action.
  • More than 50% leased or rented their premises, which can constrain decisions about major upgrades.

The survey covered 759 Australian businesses with 0–19 employees, dedicated business energy accounts and responsibility for energy decisions. It provides evidence about that population, rather than suppliers of every size.

This business perspective includes support for transition alongside concerns about implementation. Andrew McKellar, Chief Executive, Australian Chamber of Commerce and Industry, expressed it this way:

“Business looks for certainty and practical implementation to achieve a pathway toward these medium- and longer-term goals.”

Andrew McKellar, ACCI Gala Dinner address, 2025.

As a buyer you have a role in making your expectations workable. When you are requesting greenhouse gas emission information explain the purpose of a request, prioritise your material suppliers and agree the information boundary. Where a requirement change requires supplier investment, be prepared to discuss equipment-replacement cycles, financing and contract certainty.

Telstra provides an example of requests accompanied by support. In FY2025, Telstra reported that 345 suppliers shared environmental information with Telstra through the Carbon Disclosure Project’s Supply Chain program.

Three questions for leaders

  • Which supplier capabilities and commitments are essential to our own plans?
  • What constraints could prevent delivery, including finance, skills, asset ownership and timing?
  • Could a clearer specification, shared analysis or longer commitment make the response viable?
