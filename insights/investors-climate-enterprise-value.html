Investors want to understand how your business will create value

What investor expectations can tell leaders about future returns, funding and enterprise value.

Susan McHattie | Sightlines AdvisoryNovember 20262 min read (estimated)First disclosure

Explore this subject further: Which stakeholder expectations affect your business?.

An investor reviewing your business needs to understand what will sustain your earnings, where you plan for growth to come from and what might undermine their investment case. Climate-related change introduces a climate test of your commercial work, through the new climate risk disclosure requirements.

Can you explain how climate is and might change your operating conditions? Are you clear on how this affects your strategy, how and when you might need to respond? And the investment and capacity you need to put responses in place?

The Investor Group of Climate Change State of Net Zero Investment 2026 survey covered 55 institutional investors managing approximately A$3.5 trillion locally. This survey found substantial appetite for climate solutions.

  • 76% of respondents identified energy storage as an investment opportunity.
  • 71% of respondents identified renewable generation as an investment opportunity.
  • More than 25% reported allocating capital to adaptation-related solutions.

Yet, 74% identified a shortage of opportunities offering appropriate risk–return profiles, and 56% cited policy or regulatory uncertainty as a barrier.

This survey captures participating institutional investors’ views and reported practices.

“Investors see that the move to net zero is happening, but Australia can’t take their capital for granted.”

Rebecca Mikula-Wright, CEO, Investor Group on Climate Change, IGCC June 2026.

For companies seeking capital, the implication is to make the investment proposition clear. Explain the customer demand, expected returns, funding requirements and conditions on which delivery depends. Show how the business will respond if those conditions change.

The same discipline applies to protecting existing earnings. An investor may want to understand whether an exposed facility can continue operating economically, whether a product has a durable market or whether the company has sufficient financial flexibility and capacity to respond.

Three questions for leaders

  • What have our investors and lenders actually asked about our future earnings, assets and investment plans?
  • Which of our assumptions materially change under plausible climate scenarios? Would these changes shift our investor and lender perspectives?
  • Can we show how our proposed responses will affect cash flow, returns and resilience?

At Sightlines Advisory, we use the Climate Value Framework © to connect those questions to company evidence and financial analysis. We help management distinguish a promising idea from an investment proposition, identify dependencies and compare the timing of responses.
